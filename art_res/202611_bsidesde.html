Summary

Cybersecurity is not an entry-level career, and there is no tutorial level or roadmap. This talk is about what to do when you are handed a challenge you do not understand yet: how you approach it matters more than what you already know.

The core idea is one formula that works in any domain: Z = X + Y. Z is the problem or alert in front of you, X is all the data you are collecting, and Y is what you are solving for, the logic that turns X into Z. The talk frames it through Security Operations, but swap the nouns and it runs for GRC, threat hunting, or plain troubleshooting.

Agenda

  • [ 01 ]The Problem. Problems are like puzzles: a thousand pieces and no box art.
  • [ 02 ]The Formula. Same shape, every domain: Z = X + Y.
  • [ 03 ]The Gap. 90% of solving it is approach. 10% is what you already know.
  • [ 04 ]The Approach. Night City was not built in a day, and neither is your understanding.
  • [ 05 ]The Example. One real-style alert walked from start to finish.

Key Takeaways

  • Approach beats memorization. You do not need to be a subject matter expert to start. You need curiosity, patience, and humility.
  • AI is a tool, not the sole answer. Know the model, know how to prompt, and interrogate the sources instead of treating it as the source of truth.
  • Byte-size it. Build foundations first and take one piece of the puzzle at a time.
  • Ask why the alert exists. Not every event is a threat, and not every event deserves an alert. Detection logic matters when you understand why it matters.

Worked Example

The talk closes with a fictional Cyberpunk-flavored scenario: an impossible-travel alert on an executive account, taken from the alert (Z), to the raw sign-in data (X), to the detection logic (Y), adapted from a Sigma rule and converted to SPL. It ends with a bonus data exfiltration finding and the lessons learned. All data in the scenario is simulated.
